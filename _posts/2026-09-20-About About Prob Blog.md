# About Prob Blog

## Our Goal

The goal of Prob Blog is three-fold: to encourage human-generated mathematics content, to provide intuitive and introductory explanations for people seeking to learn more about probability, and to offer opportunities for young mathematicians to learn and explain new or familiar mathematical topics.     


## Why Human-generated?
Lately, mathematics has become a playing ground for large companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others, whose interest in mathematics is driven exclusively by their desire to showcase the ability of some new model. Our position is quite simple: AI-generated mathematics content at its current growth rate has the potential to destroy the discipline. We will avoid writing an essay here, as "The crisis of AI-generated mathematics" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02859) makes many of our ideas explicit, but the general point is that if we follow the current trajectory, human understanding of mathematics and thus its usefulness will rapidly be destroyed. I, for one, don't want that, and thus this blog is a small effort towards making sure humans keep doing mathematics, and humans keep talking about it! For those in research, I strongly recommend reading about and joining The Association for Human Mathematics (AHM); see: https://www.ahmath.org/. 

## Why Probability?

Probability as a formal discipline is relatively new, largely driven by Kolmogorov in 1933, however its complete history dates back to the gambling questions studied by Pascal. In todays world, probability theory is everywhere including, but not limited to: the theory behind Large-language models (ironically), finance, physics, chemistry, biology, number theory, and statistics. With probability theory we can say an awful lot about a system or object which is random and hence inherently unpredictable. And once you see it in our chaotic world it is difficult to see anything other random processes. This is in some foundational sense true if we consider that Quantum mechanics is fundamentally a probabilistic theory.
